A salesperson's whole job, in three parts. Most of the day-to-day behaviors behind them live in [[Sales call rules]], and the numbers in [[Sales funnel metrics]].

## 1. Maximize the number of opportunities (leads)
- **Total hours available per day** is the greatest lever on throughput.
- **Pull calls forward:** fill time slots with as many calls as possible. Moving a Wednesday appointment to Monday also shows you their interest and intent.
- **Two standard procedures:** what you do on the call, and what you do off the call (follow-up, high activity).

## 2. Convert the highest percentage of those opportunities
- Book a meeting from a meeting (BAMFAM): always know exactly when you'll next talk.
- Don't take rejection personally: great salespeople get more nos than mediocre ones. It's a numbers game.
- Kill list: the high-value prospects that get extra attention.
- Research a prospect for as long as the call will last (1-hour call, 1 hour of research).
- Always ask for referrals: "Who do you know that's as awesome as you and would be interested in this?" Referrals close at 80–90%.
- Take notes; notes are prep for the next call.
- Never blame circumstances: focus on what you control.

## 3. Do it consistently for a very long time
The first two jobs only pay off with consistency. See [[Salesperson mindset]] and [[Daily sales training]].

## Scale
- Selling expensive things: simplicity is what scales.
- The notes say: "If 100k>/month, it's just you. If 1M+/month, you need people." Likely meaning: up to about $100k/month the founder can sell alone; at $1M+/month you need a team. Open question #4 in [[Open questions - Hormozi notes]].
