Method for handling an objection or pushback: find the pressure underneath instead of arguing the words. Tactical empathy means showing you understand how they got there; it is not agreeing and not sympathy.

## Steps
1. **Pause.** Silently ask "what made them say that?" Do not defend.
2. **Label.** Name the likely feeling or pressure as a guess: "Seems like...", "Sounds like...". Then stop talking. If they correct you, that is useful information.
3. **Mirror.** Repeat the last one to three words they said, with curiosity. Then stay quiet.
4. **Dynamic silence.** Hold the silence. People under pressure often say the real reason to fill it.
5. **Ask one open question** about the real issue once they confirm the label. Examples from the post: "How are you being asked to justify this internally?"
6. **Label yourself first** if you are triggered, then choose your response.

## Rules
- Do not explain or correct before they finish; it reads as talking down.
- Works best when there is real pressure (price, plan, results challenged), not in polite talk.
- It uncovers the issue; it does not close the deal by itself.

- "Es muy caro." Mirror: "¿Muy caro?" Silence. Label: "Suena a que lo difícil es justificar el gasto con los socios." See [[Label the pressure behind a price objection]].
- Receptionist resists the new process: "Parece que sientes que esto te quita control sobre tus pacientes."

## Why it holds elsewhere (shared rule)
A doctor treats the cause behind the complaint, not the words (diagnosis before prescription). Journalists and police use silence the same way: unfilled silence creates mild pressure the other person resolves by giving information. Naming an emotion creates a gap between impulse and action, which is why pilots and athletes do it.
