Takeaway: when two sides must agree without a referee, they pick whatever stands out as obvious. Whoever sets the frame first decides what stands out.

## Rules
1. Name the comparison first. The first thing the owner compares us to becomes the default.
2. Use round, qualitative lines: "one fixed price", "30 days", "no setup fee", not graded amounts.
3. Words change the frame: "dues" vs "taxes" got different answers in Schelling's tests. Say "booked consultations", not "leads".
4. Do not accept a bad default just because it is conventional. If clinics expect to pay per month for "marketing", say what they are really buying.
5. The weaker party usually takes the stronger party's frame. In the first meeting the doctor is the expert in medicine; we set the frame only on patient messages and bookings, our gold medal.

## Clinic scripts (drafts, Spanish to be reviewed)
- Set the comparison: "No compárenos con una agencia de marketing; compárenos con la persona de recepción que necesitaría para cubrir los mismos horarios." English: "Do not compare us with a marketing agency; compare us with the front-desk person you would need to cover the same hours."
- Name the stopping point: "Le damos una sola bonificación: la reactivación de su lista. Después de eso el trato es el que está en la hoja." English: "We give one bonus only: reactivating your list. After that the deal is the one on the sheet."

## Why it holds elsewhere (shared rule)
Without a referee, people coordinate on the most salient option: a meeting place and time for strangers, a round price, a bright rule such as "no sugar". Make yours the salient one.

## Limits
Secondary source; Schelling's experiments were with small groups and a few options, not price negotiations. Effect with clinic owners: `unknown`.
