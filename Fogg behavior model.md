A behavior happens only when motivation, ability and a prompt arrive at the same moment (B = M + A + P). If it did not happen, one of the three is missing. Find which one before you change anything.

## The three parts
- **Motivation.** Three pairs: pleasure or pain, hope or fear, social acceptance or rejection. It rises and falls, so do not rely on it. When it is high people do hard things; when it dips they do only easy ones.
- **Ability (simplicity).** How easy it is for this person right now. Three ways to raise it: training (hardest, avoid), a tool or resource, or shrinking the behavior (preferred). The scarcest resource (time, money) is the weakest link. The names of the individual ability factors are `unknown` here (page text says five, others six).
- **Prompt.** The cue that says "do it now": a message, alarm or routine. Types: Facilitator, Signal, Spark. Only Facilitator (a prompt that makes the action easy) is explained on the pages read; Signal and Spark are `unknown` here.

Motivation and ability trade off: very high motivation can overcome a hard step; a very easy step happens even with low motivation.

## Diagnose in this order
1. Did the person get a prompt? If not, add one.
2. Was the step easy for them right now? If not, shrink it or give a tool.
3. Only then work on motivation.

## Build a behavior chain
Map the chain of behaviors you want, get the first small one, design the next step if people do not take it, repeat. Never ask for a complicated behavior in one prompt.

## Why it holds elsewhere (shared rule)
Fire needs fuel, heat and oxygen; remove one and it stops. Any result that needs several conditions at once is controlled by finding the missing one. Also: set defaults so the desired action needs no daily decision (automatic savings), and fix the scarcest constraint first (factory bottleneck).
