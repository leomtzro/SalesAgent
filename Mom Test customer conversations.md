How to talk to a prospect and get true answers. People lie to be kind, so ask about their life and the past, not about your idea and the future. Use it on discovery calls with clinic owners and in the intake questions of the patient agent.

## Key takeaways
1. Talk about their life, not your idea. Do not pitch on a discovery call.
2. Ask about specific past events ("la última vez que...") instead of opinions or promises about the future.
3. Talk less, listen more, write down exact words and numbers.
4. Compliments ("qué buena idea"), fluff ("siempre", "nunca", "lo compraría") and hypotheticals are not data. Pull back to one real case.
5. Do not build or promise what they ask for. Ask what problem it solves and how they handle it today.
6. If they have not looked for a solution or paid for one, they will probably not buy ours.
7. Every call ends with a commitment and a next step. See [[Commitment ladder]].
8. A refused ask is information, not failure.

## Questions to use
General (the book's patterns, from the reviews):
- "Talk me through the last time X happened."
- "What else have you tried? What did it cost you in time and money?"
- "Why do you bother with X?" / "What are the implications of that?"
- "What are your top 3 priorities right now?"
- "Why do you want that?" (for any feature request)
- "Where does the budget come from?"
- Closers: "Who else should I talk to?" and "Is there anything else I should have asked?"

Questions to avoid: "Would you buy this?", "Do you think it's a good idea?", "How much would you pay?"

Clinic version in Spanish (adapted, untested): the full list is in [[Clinic discovery question bank]]. The three to start with:
1. "Cuéntame de la última vez que un paciente nuevo escribió por WhatsApp y no llegó a la cita. ¿Qué pasó exactamente?"
2. "¿Qué han probado antes? ¿Cuánto les costó y qué pasó?"
3. "¿Quién decide el gasto, y de dónde sale ese presupuesto?"

## Why it holds elsewhere (shared rule)
Past behavior beats stated intent in hiring interviews. Costly signals beat words in fundraising (a term sheet vs "we love it"). Doctors ask "what are you trying to do?" before prescribing what the patient asks for. Shared rule: what someone did, paid or committed predicts more than what they say.

## Limits
The book was not read; this is from two reviews. Exact book wording is `unknown`. Reviewers note that interviews test customer risk, not technical risk, and that the no-pitch rule sits awkwardly with asking for commitments. Spanish phrasing and fit with Tijuana business politeness are `unknown` until tested on real calls.
