The beliefs that make selling work. Everything else in Sales builds on these.

**Rule of thumb:** buy time by buying the lessons other people learned from their mistakes. Not doing so is the "time tax of ignorance."

**Emotional vs. logical buyers**
- Emotionally, people want to believe you and want to buy.
- Logically, you must find the way to help them justify the decision.

## The beliefs
1. People want to buy (emotionally), but you have to help them justify the decision (logically).
2. Selling happens before you ask for the sale; closing happens after you ask for the sale.
3. It's easier to handle obstacles than objections.
   - **Obstacle:** something that blocks progress *before* the sale.
   - **Objection:** disapproval or opposition *after* you ask, often when they disagree with something you said. Example: "I need to think about it" → "Great, what is it that you need to think about?"
4. Expect and plan for no. No is the job.
5. If you didn't get a gasp on the price tag, you didn't go high enough.
6. Selling properly is the first step in becoming a coach: your first impression and the expectations you set dictate the relationship.
7. Selling is helping prospects make decisions to help themselves.
8. Keep the prospect, not the sale, as the priority. The more you vanish and magnify them, the better you do.
9. Seek to understand, not to argue. When someone objects, say "I wouldn't have thought about that" instead of attacking the objection.
10. Closing is a dance, not a fight; you don't beat them into submission.
11. Selling is the transference of belief over a bridge of trust. You must believe in order to transfer belief, and you need trust to make the transfer. The customer must want the goal and believe:
    1. The product will get them there the way they want to go.
    2. You and others will support them.
    3. It will work for them, not just for everyone else.
    4. Give enough proof that not believing looks more unreasonable than believing.
    5. Use a "postcard from the future" (a picture of their life after the result).
12. You can only build trust if you genuinely want to help; people sniff out intention.
13. Belief and trust are continuums, not yes/no. There are levels.
14. Closers ask hard questions because they genuinely care.
15. The person who cares most about the prospect wins the sale.
16. Record all your sales calls, always, especially when you're on a hot streak.
17. Power is the ability to direct or influence people.

## When does selling happen?
At the lead, while qualifying, when asking for the sale, when closing: all the time. Closing is the highest predictor of success in business.

**The 10/80/10 rule:** of 100 people who walk in, about 10% will never buy and 10% will always buy (you just can't mess it up). You fight for the 80% in the middle. You don't know which 10% is which.

**How to treat prospects:** "I don't want you to buy; I want you to decide." By this standard, you should be happy when people say no: they decided.
